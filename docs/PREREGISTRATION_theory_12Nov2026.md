# Pre-registration: a closed-form theory of the closure share (12 Nov 2026, written before the code was run)

Purpose: move the macrocycle paper from "a diagnostic and a refuted mechanism" toward a theory with testable predictions. Nothing below has been computed yet.

## Derivation (mean-field, high-noise limit, bond restraints only)
Take a ring of n atoms, harmonic restraints on the n-1 sequential bonds and the closure bond, same force constant k, and coordinates that are independent isotropic noise (diffusion at t = 1). When the noised bond length is much larger than b0, each bond force is approximately c d (c = 2k), where d is the bond vector. A bond vector has per-coordinate variance 2 sigma^2, so a single bond force F has |F|^2 = c^2 6 sigma^2.
- The closure term touches two atoms, each with force magnitude |F|, so ||g_closure|| = sqrt(2) |F|.
- An interior atom carries the sum of two bond forces whose bond vectors share noise with opposite sign: c (2 e_i - e_{i-1} - e_{i+1}), squared magnitude 3 |F|^2. The two chain ends carry |F|^2 each. So ||g_bond||^2 = (3(n-2) + 2) |F|^2 = (3n - 4) |F|^2.
Predictions (T1): f_cl = sqrt(2) / (sqrt(2) + sqrt(3n - 4)) for bond terms only; per-bond enrichment E(n) = sqrt(2) (n-1) / sqrt(3n - 4). Scaling the closure restraint by a factor lambda gives f_cl(lambda) = lambda sqrt(2) / (lambda sqrt(2) + sqrt(3n - 4)), so the closure restraint dominates (f_cl = 0.5) only when lambda* = sqrt((3n - 4) / 2) (T2; 4.7 for n = 16). Corollary: with equal restraint strengths no ring of more than two atoms can have f_cl above 0.5.

## Test (SIMULATION on synthetic rings; labelled simulation)
Synthetic rings (phase1_closure.synthetic_ring), n in {8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48}; noise at t = 1 with scale = radius of gyration; k_angle = 0, k_steric = 0, no interface term; 400 draws per ring size; seed 20261130.
Rules (failure reported as failure): (RT1) measured mean f_cl is within 10% (relative) of the T1 prediction in at least 5 of 6 ring sizes; (RT2) the lambda at which the measured mean f_cl crosses 0.5 (bisection, 400 draws per evaluation) is within 15% of sqrt((3n-4)/2) in at least 5 of 6 ring sizes; (RT3) per-bond enrichment within 10% of E(n) in at least 5 of 6 sizes. Reported, not rules: the same comparison at t = 0.5 (not covered by the high-noise derivation), and the five deposited structures (already analysed on 13 Oct, so this is a post-hoc description, not a test) with bond terms only at t = 1.
Expectation stated in advance, not a result: the derivation ignores the spread in c across bonds, so small rings (n = 8) may deviate by more than 10%.

## Result (12 Nov 2026, SIMULATION; 6 ring sizes, 400 draws each, seed 20261130; scripts/phase4_theory.py, results/phase4_theory.csv)
RT1 NOT met: measured f_cl was within 10% of the prediction in 4 of 6 sizes (errors -8.9% to -12.0%; always below the prediction). RT2 NOT met: the measured lambda* was 21% to 40% above sqrt((3n-4)/2) (for example 5.9 against 4.7 at n = 16). RT3 met: per-bond enrichment was within 10% of E(n) in 6 of 6 sizes (errors -6.2% to -9.4%). The measured f_cl fell with ring size at the predicted rate (log-log slope -0.44 against -0.45 predicted), so the n^(-1/2) law holds, with a roughly constant 10% over-prediction. At t = 0.5 the values were almost the same as at t = 1, as expected for a flat share. Reading: the scaling law and the corollary (no ring dominates at equal strength) hold; the absolute constant is not exact, because the derivation takes the bond stiffness as constant while the force on a noised bond depends on its length. The deposited structures were not re-run for this note (not needed for the rules above).

## Amendment 2 (12 Nov 2026, written before theory v2 and the open-chain experiments were run)
**Theory v2.** The Amendment-1 derivation treated bond stiffness as constant. The force on a bond of length r is 2k (1 - b0/r) d, so the coefficient c = 1 - b0/r depends on the noised length, and neighbouring bonds share noise. Define, for three consecutive atoms with independent isotropic noise of per-coordinate standard deviation sigma, d_a = e_1 - e_0 and d_b = e_2 - e_1, A = E[c(d_a)^2 |d_a|^2] and B = E[c(d_a) c(d_b) d_a . d_b], rho = B / A (computed by Monte Carlo, 4 million draws, as a function of sigma / b0 only; with constant c it equals -1/2 and gives Amendment 1). Then ||g_closure||^2 = 2A, ||g_bond||^2 = A (2 (n-2) (1 - rho) + 2) = A Q, so f_cl = sqrt(2) / (sqrt(2) + sqrt(Q)), per-bond enrichment E = sqrt(2) (n-1) / sqrt(Q), and lambda* = sqrt((n-2)(1 - rho) + 1). sigma is the radius of gyration of the ring (as in the noising), not fitted. New test: ring sizes {10, 14, 20, 28, 40, 64}, 400 draws, seed 20261201, bond terms only, t = 1. Rules: (RT4) f_cl within 5% of the v2 prediction in at least 5 of 6 sizes; (RT5) measured lambda* within 10% in at least 5 of 6; (RT6) enrichment within 5% in at least 5 of 6.

**Open-chain regime (a scope check on the 13 Oct refutation; not a rescue).** The 13 Oct result was measured on noised closed rings, so the closure bond was no more violated than the others. A generated backbone whose ends have not met has the other bonds intact and a closure bond far from 3.8 A. The published 0% baseline could belong to that regime. Test S1: random-walk chains (CA-CA 3.8 A, uniformly random directions, so the closure distance is large and every sequential bond is exactly 3.8 A), n in {12, 16, 24}, 200 chains per size, seed 20261202, full energy (bond, closure, angle, steric; no interface), lambda = 1. Rule (RS1, decides the scope of the refutation): the closure share exceeds 0.5 for the mean of at least 2 of 3 sizes. Whatever the result, it is reported as the boundary of the earlier refutation, and the earlier 13 Oct numbers stand for the regime in which they were measured.
**Share-capped schedule (a method).** In the annealing toy of Experiment B (2,000 steps, step 5e-4, decaying noise), start from the same open chains and compare three schedules for the closure weight: constant lambda = 1; the sigmoid 'aware' schedule of the 13 Oct code; and 'capped', which at every step sets lambda so that the closure term's share of the summed gradient norm is at most q = 0.2 (lambda = min(1, q / (1 - q) x (sum of the other terms' norms) / ||g_closure||)), a rule derived from the theory above (the share of an equally weighted closure bond in a closed ring is about 0.1 to 0.2). 100 runs per size and schedule, seed 20261203. Rules: (RS2) capped closes the ring (final closure distance below 5 A) in at least 90% of runs at every size; (RS3) capped has a lower mean final CA-CA bond RMS deviation than constant at every size; (RS4) capped's closure success rate is at least that of the sigmoid schedule minus 5 points at every size. Reported, not rules: final angle strain, final closure distance and RMSD between schedules. Expectation stated in advance, not a result: closure may fail for the capped schedule if the cap is too low; if so that will be reported.

## Amendment 3 (12 Nov 2026, written before theory v3 was run)
**Theory v3.** The v1 prediction replaces random norms by their expectations. The closure force magnitude |F| = s chi, where chi is chi-distributed with 3 degrees of freedom (a Gaussian bond vector's length) and E|F|^2 = 3 s^2; the bond-term norm sums many atoms and is close to its expectation, ||g_bond|| = sqrt((3n - 4) 3) s. The closure norm is sqrt(2) lambda s chi. So f_cl = E_chi[ sqrt(2) lambda chi / (sqrt(2) lambda chi + sqrt(3 (3n - 4))) ], evaluated by numerical integration, with enrichment E_enr = sqrt(2) (n-1) E[chi] / sqrt(3 (3n - 4)) (E[chi] = 2 sqrt(2/pi) = 1.596 against sqrt(3) = 1.732 for v1, a factor 0.921), and lambda* the root of f_cl(lambda) = 0.5. Jensen's inequality (the share is concave in the closure norm) predicts v1 over-states f_cl, over-states enrichment by about 8%, and under-states lambda*, the direction seen in Experiments 1 and 2. Test: ring sizes {9, 13, 18, 26, 36, 56}, 400 draws, bond terms only, t = 1, seed 20261204. Rules: (RT7) f_cl within 5% of v3 in at least 5 of 6 sizes; (RT8) lambda* within 10% in at least 5 of 6; (RT9) enrichment within 5% in at least 5 of 6.

## Result of Amendment 3, theory v3 (12 Nov 2026, SIMULATION; sizes 9 to 56, seed 20261204; scripts/phase6_theory_v3.py, results/phase6_theory_v3.csv)
RT7 met (f_cl within 5% of v3 in 6 of 6 sizes; errors -3.2% to +1.3%), RT9 met (enrichment within 5% in 6 of 6; errors -0.2% to +1.9%), RT8 met (lambda* within 10% in 5 of 6; the exception n = 9 was 12.1% high). The bias of v1 and v2 was a Jensen effect of treating the closure norm, a chi-distributed quantity, as its mean. Provenance: v3 was derived after two failed tests on the same simulation setting and tested on new ring sizes and a new seed; v1 and v2 failures stand as reported.

## Amendment 4 (13 Nov 2026, 06:20 IST, written before the multi-restraint test was run)
**Theory v4: several closure restraints (disulfide and bicycle chemistries).** v3 treated one closure restraint. A disulfide or bicyclic macrocycle carries m closure restraints on m disjoint atom pairs, each with its own native equilibrium length. In the same high-noise approximation each restraint force has magnitude s chi_j (chi_j chi-distributed, 3 degrees of freedom, independent because the atoms are disjoint), and the closure-group gradient norm is sqrt(2) lambda s sqrt(sum_j chi_j^2) = sqrt(2) lambda s chi_(3m), where chi_(3m) has 3m degrees of freedom. The bond group is unchanged (n-1 sequential bonds, ||g_bond|| = sqrt(3 (3n-4)) s). Prediction (T4): f_cl(n, m, lambda) = E[ sqrt(2) lambda chi_(3m) / (sqrt(2) lambda chi_(3m) + sqrt(3 (3n-4))) ], and lambda*(n, m) is its root at 0.5. Consequences stated before testing: f_cl grows with m (roughly as sqrt(m) while f_cl is small), and closure dominates at lambda = 1 only for m beyond roughly (3n-4)/2 independent restraints.
**Test (SIMULATION, synthetic rings).** n in {18, 30}, m in {1, 2, 3, 4}; closure restraints: the pair (n-1, 0) plus m-1 further disjoint non-adjacent random pairs, each restraint's equilibrium length equal to the pair's distance in the synthetic ring; k_angle = 0, k_steric = 0, no interface term; t = 1, scale = radius of gyration, 400 draws per cell; seed 20261205. Rules: (RT10) measured f_cl within 5% of T4 in at least 7 of 8 cells; (RT11) measured lambda* within 10% of T4 in at least 6 of 8 cells; (RT12) in every ring size, measured f_cl is strictly increasing in m.
Reported, not rules: (a) the predicted m at which f_cl = 0.5 for n = 14 and n = 29; (b) the T4 predictions for the real chemistries (n, m from the 13 Oct structures) next to the measured values of experiment B1 (bond, angle, steric and interface terms all present, so this comparison is post-hoc and descriptive, and cannot count as a test).
Expectation stated in advance, not a result: the approximation ignores the fact that restraints with long native distances (disulfide pairs 4 to 7 A apart in a CA model) are less stretched than restraints of 3.8 A, so m > 1 cells may deviate by more than the m = 1 cell.

## Amendment 6 (13 Nov 2026, written before theory v5 was run)
**Theory v5: native-length correction.** The force of restraint j with native length b_j on a noised pair of length r_j is 2k |r_j - b_j|, not 2k r_j. At t = 1 the noised coordinates are s_0 N(0, 1) with s_0 = radius of gyration, so a pair vector has per-coordinate standard deviation s = sqrt(2) s_0 and r_j = s chi_j (chi_j with 3 degrees of freedom). Predictions (T5), using only n, the ring's own radius of gyration and the restraints' own native lengths (nothing fitted): closure-group norm a = sqrt(2) lambda sqrt( sum_j (s chi_j - b_j)^2 ); bond-group norm B = sqrt( (3n - 4) E[(s chi - 3.8)^2] ); f_cl = E[ a / (a + B) ], lambda* the root of f_cl = 0.5. For b_j = 3.8 this reduces to v3 up to the replacement of s chi by |s chi - 3.8|. Test (SIMULATION), new sizes n in {22, 40}, m in {1, 2, 3, 4}, same restraint-placement rule as Amendment 4, 400 draws, seed 20261207. Rules: (RT13) f_cl within 5% of T5 in at least 7 of 8 cells; (RT14) lambda* within 10% in at least 6 of 8; (RT15) T5 improves on T4 (smaller mean absolute error in f_cl) over the 8 cells. ## Amendment 8 (13 Nov 2026, written before any latent computation): closure share in the CP-Composer autoencoder latent space
Question: does the Cartesian result (closure share below 0.2 in noised closed rings) survive when the gradient is taken with respect to the latent code of the published CP-Composer (PepGLAD-derived) autoencoder?
Method: encode each of the nine deposited structures with the published autoencoder (checkpoint `checkpoints/autoencoder.pth` of github.com/jdp22/CP-Composer_final, CPU, no_randomness), add forward-diffusion noise to the latent at t in {0.3, 0.45, 0.6, 0.75, 0.9} (cosine schedule as in the Cartesian test), decode, take the decoded C-alpha coordinates, evaluate the same molecular-mechanics terms as Section 2.4 (closure, bond, angle, steric), and obtain each term's latent gradient by autograd through the decoder. Latent closure share = ||grad_z E_closure|| / sum over terms ||grad_z E_term||, averaged over 32 draws per time and over t.
Rules (same bands as the Cartesian rule): above 0.5 closure dominates in latent space; 0.2 to 0.5 partial; below 0.2 refuted in latent space. Secondary: cosine between the latent-projected and Cartesian closure gradient (descriptive only).
Reported whatever the outcome. Limits fixed in advance: autoencoder only (not the denoiser), the energy is our molecular-mechanics energy and not the energy used in CP-Composer's own baseline, nine structures, CPU.

Amendment 8, details fixed before the first latent run (13 Nov 2026): (i) all nine structures are scored with the head-to-tail closure topology and no target (the autoencoder is run on the peptide alone, all residues generated); (ii) the latent is the autoencoder's 3-D latent coordinate Z per residue, noised with the cosine schedule on centred coordinates at scale equal to the radius of gyration of Z, and the hidden latent H noised with standard Gaussian noise by the same alpha-bar; the primary gradient is with respect to Z, the gradient with respect to H is reported as secondary; (iii) the decoder is run with the true sequence; (iv) each term's latent gradient is the vector-Jacobian product of the decoder with the analytic Cartesian gradient of that term (the same energy functions as Section 2.4) taken on the decoded C-alpha; (v) a Cartesian reference share without target is computed on the native C-alpha with the same draws and times, so latent and Cartesian shares are compared like for like; (vi) structures: 9CDT, 9CDU, 9HGC, 9HGD, 9CDZ (peptide chain B) from the 1 Jan 2026 wwPDB snapshot and 1SFI (chain I), 1JBL, 1NB1, 1K48 (chain A) from the same snapshot; (vii) seed 20261209.

## Amendment 9 (13 Nov 2026, written before any sampler run): closure share along trajectories of the full CP-Composer denoiser
Question: along the reverse-diffusion trajectory of the published CP-Composer (PepGLAD-derived) latent denoiser, with energy guidance as in its published energy-guided baseline, does the ring-closure term dominate the guidance gradient?
Model: `checkpoints/version_325/checkpoint/epoch37_step85234.ckpt` of github.com/jdp22/CP-Composer_final (the authors' trained Prompt_LDMPepDesign with its autoencoder), CPU, nothing trained by us. Classifier-free prompt guidance is switched off (w = -1) so that only energy guidance acts; this is the energy-guided baseline of the paper, and CP-Composer's own prompt guidance is not tested.
Design: for each of the five complexes 9CDT, 9CDU, 9HGC, 9HGD, 9CDZ (1 Jan 2026 wwPDB snapshot) the receptor pocket (receptor residues with any atom within 8 A of the peptide, at most 60 residues) is the context and a peptide of the native length is generated, 100 reverse steps, 4 samples per complex and condition, seed 20261210. Conditions: (U) unguided, energy_lambda 0, shares recorded only; (G1) guided by the authors' closure term alone (condition2, head to tail latent distance, upper bound 3.5), lambda 30; (G2) guided by closure plus the authors' default geometry terms (consecutive-distance, inner clash, outer clash), lambda 30.
Measures at every step: (a) share of the gradient norm with respect to the generated latent coordinates carried by the closure term out of closure + consecutive + inner clash + outer clash (the authors' own energy components); (b) the same share for our molecular-mechanics terms (closure, bond, angle, steric on the decoded C-alpha, pulled back through the decoder by the vector-Jacobian product, as in Amendment 8). Both are averaged over steps 30 to 90 of 100 (the window t in [0.3, 0.9]) and over samples.
Rules (same bands as Section 2.4, applied to measure (b), the primary): above 0.5 closure dominates along sampler trajectories; 0.2 to 0.5 partial; below 0.2 refuted. Measure (a) is descriptive. Outcome: final decoded C-alpha head-to-tail distance below 5 A counted as closed. Limits fixed in advance: no prompt guidance, peptide chains generated without sequence design constraints, our energy for (b), 20 samples per condition, CPU, pocket context from deposited complexes, no claim about the published 0% success rate.

### Amendment 9 result (13 Nov 2026, `benchmark/scripts/phase11_sampler_closure.py`, `results/phase11_sampler_closure.csv`, `phase11_sampler_steps.csv`, seed 20261210, CPU, 5 complexes x 3 conditions x 4 samples = 60 trajectories)
Primary (b), our MM closure share through the decoder, steps 30 to 90, mean over 20 trajectories per condition: unguided 0.131 (per-trajectory 0.077 to 0.215; per-complex means 0.114 to 0.148), closure-only guidance 0.130 (0.070 to 0.194), closure plus geometry guidance 0.352 (0.168 to 0.502; per-complex means 0.291 to 0.440). Limits as fixed: no prompt guidance, pocket context, our energy, 20 samples per condition, CPU, no statement about the published 0% success rate.
